# Whisk & Fury: Recipe Rumble — design brief

- Player fantasy / audience / orientation / session length: Be a charismatic chef in a spectacular underground cooking tournament, defeating animated living recipes and rival chefs through skillful ingredient combos. Broad adult casual audience with women 35–45 as the first research cohort, never a stereotype; portrait; 7–12 minute runs and 60–90 second encounters.
- One-sentence core loop and first meaningful action: Read the enemy recipe's visible flavor sequence, fling matching animated ingredients to complete it, use techniques to defend or amplify the combo, then collect a new card or kitchen tool and choose the next culinary arena. The first action throws a tomato into a furious pasta monster and visibly knocks it backward in a sauce burst.
- First 10-minute path: branded animated splash; optional skippable cooking lesson; play a guaranteed Guard-bearing Fresh, Heat, and Rich hand; see each correct match advance the recipe; trigger the first Perfect Plate; use accumulated Guard to absorb the first boss hit; continue the fight independently; transform the monster into a plated dish; choose one of three illustrated cards; discover the Cookbook and preview later bosses.
- Controls, accessibility/comfort, and feedback design: Large tap cards and explicit flavor icons; no drag requirement; intent copy plus animation; readable card text; reduced-motion substitutes for camera shake/arcs; separate music/SFX/haptics; every impact has visual, sound, state, and text feedback.
- Difficulty/pacing/content cadence and skill/RNG policy: Three Heat per turn, five-card hand, transparent enemy intent and flavor sequence, deterministic effects, seeded draw. RNG presents choices, not surprise punishment. Recipes gain layered patterns and counters; rival chefs alter arena rules. Quick retry and guaranteed Cookbook progress keep failure emotionally fair.
- Economy sources/sinks/caps and non-payer promise: Run-only Tickets buy cards or upgrades at market nodes and reset after the tournament. Cookbook Stars unlock new starting recipes, cosmetic restaurant displays, and alternate hero tools. Every encounter, card, route, boss, and progression path is playable without payment or ads.
- Return reason and LiveOps/content seams: Fill the animated Cookbook, restore the hero's restaurant trophy wall, discover new recipe monsters, and attempt weekly seeded tasting menus. Stable IDs cover cards, flavor tags, enemies, arenas, tools, routes, and cosmetic sets.
- FTUE, core-loop, progression-stall, retention, and trust metrics: lesson step viewed, skipped/completed, first correct flavor, first Perfect Plate, first guarded hit, first transformed dish, first card choice, boss reach/win, retry, run duration, sequence completion, Cookbook milestone, D1/D7, errors, and post-offer abandonment. No demographic or sensitive properties. The tutorial completion flag is durable appStorage progress, and the lesson is always replayable from the menu.
- Vertical slice, player-test plan, owner, and next decision date: One hero, three living recipes, one rival chef boss, 24 cards, two arenas, a complete short run, visible transformations, particles, save/reload, and settings. Test with 5–8 representative adult players after animation/art integration; expand only after the throw/combo loop reads without explanation.

## Mechanical pillars

1. **Throw it:** every ingredient card becomes a visible projectile with anticipation, arc, impact, enemy reaction, splatter/steam particles, and updated flavor sequence.
2. **Cook the pattern:** complete flavor sequences such as `FRESH → HEAT → RICH` for a large technique animation and recipe transformation.
3. **Outplay the kitchen:** use Chop, Toss, Season, Flambé, Chill, and Plate techniques to duplicate tags, rearrange sequences, generate defense, or exploit enemy intent.
4. **Make something beautiful:** a defeated food monster does not die; it resolves into an appetizing plated dish with a celebratory reveal.
5. **Face the chef:** rival bosses have expressive 2D character animation and manipulate recipes, steal ingredients, raise the heat, or counter repeated flavors.
